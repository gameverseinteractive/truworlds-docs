# Publishing your game

Releasing a game takes two places: TruWorlds Creator publishes your world into a [project](universes-and-worlds.md) on TruWorlds, and the Create site sets the project's details, age rating and visibility. Nobody can play a project until its Starting World has been published, whatever its visibility.

## Publishing from TruWorlds Creator

To publish, click Publish on the right of the ribbon's tab row, or choose File > Publish to TruWorlds... You need to be signed in to publish a world. See [Saving and publishing](../creator/saving-and-publishing.md#publishing-to-truworlds).

Publishing first asks you to choose a project: pick one of your projects, or New project..., then click Next.

If you chose New project..., type a Name (up to 80 characters) and, if you like, a Description, and click Publish. A new project is created with this world in it. The world you publish to New project... becomes the new project's Starting World.

If you chose one of your projects, choose a world: pick the world in that project to publish over and click Publish, or pick New world... and click Next. The list marks which world is the project's Starting World.

If you chose New world..., type a Name (up to 80 characters) and click Publish. A project holds up to 2 worlds, or up to 10 if its owner has a TruWorlds membership.

Publishing to New project... or New world... first creates the project or world, then publishes your world into it.

If publishing to New project... fails after the project was created, publish again and choose that project instead of New project...

A Published message confirms the publish: `<World> in <Project> was published at <time>.` To keep the file linked, see [Saving and publishing](../creator/saving-and-publishing.md#publishing-to-truworlds).

## Your project on the Create site

The Create site is the TruWorlds website where you manage your projects. It is at create.truworlds.com. Your projects are listed under Projects on the Create site. Click one to open its settings.

On the Create site, a project's tabs are General, Worlds, Servers, Media, Analytics and Store. View on site, at the top of a project's page, opens the page players see.

## Filling in the details

A project's General tab holds Identity (Name, and Description up to 2000 characters), Access (Visibility and Age rating) and Discovery (Genre and Tags).

Under Discovery on the General tab, choose a Genre, and click Add tags to choose up to 5 Tags.

The Media tab sets the project's Thumbnail (16:9) and Icon (square), each a PNG, JPEG or WebP image of up to 5 MB.

## The age rating

An age rating must be set before a project can be made Public. An age rating helps make sure your game reaches an appropriate age range.

To set the age rating, open the project's General tab on the Create site and click Set rating under Age rating, in the Access section. Answer every category of the questionnaire, then click Submit.

The age rating questionnaire has seven categories: Violence, Blood & Gore, Weapons, Horror & Fear, Language, Substances, and Nature of Player Interaction. In each, choose the option that best describes your project, or its most severe content.

Your most mature answer sets the project's rating, which is Everyone, 10+ or 13+.

To change your answers later, click Edit under Age rating. The questionnaire opens with your earlier answers filled in.

TruWorlds may adjust age ratings at any time, and may lock an age rating that is set incorrectly.

## Who can play

A project's Visibility is Private ("Only you can join."), Unlisted ("Anyone can join, not publicly discoverable.") or Public ("Anyone can join, publicly discoverable."). A new project's visibility is Private. You can change a project's visibility at any time, including making a Public game Unlisted or Private again. The worlds of a project share visibility and age rating, set once on the General tab.

To let other players in, publish the project's Starting World and set its Visibility to Unlisted or Public.

## Publishing updates

To update your game, open the world's file in TruWorlds Creator and publish it again to the same world. Earlier published versions of a world are kept when you publish over it.

New servers start with the newest published version of a world. After you publish a new version of a world, servers that were already running keep the version they started with.

## Moving players onto an update

The Servers tab lets you move players onto the new version without waiting for the old servers to empty.

To restart servers for updates, open the project and click the Servers tab (its section is titled Restart servers for updates). Under World, choose All worlds or one world. Under Delay, choose how long players are warned for. Click Restart, then click Restart to confirm.

Restart servers for updates needs a newer published version: only servers that run an older version restart. After the delay, players see a notice and move, still in the game, to a server of the Starting World that runs the newest published version.

Use Restart servers for updates rather than shutting servers down, because it keeps players playing. For how a server shutting down looks to players, see [Handling shutdowns](../players/handling-shutdowns.md).

## Troubleshooting

| What you see | Fix |
|---|---|
| Other players can't join your game. | Publish the project's Starting World and set its Visibility to Unlisted or Public: [Who can play](#who-can-play). |
| You can't make your project Public. | Set the age rating: [The age rating](#the-age-rating). |
| Players are still on the old version after you published. | Restart servers for updates: [Publishing updates](#publishing-updates), [Moving players onto an update](#moving-players-onto-an-update). |
| Publishing to New project... failed, but the project is on the Create site. | Publish again and choose that project instead of New project...: [Publishing from TruWorlds Creator](#publishing-from-truworlds-creator). |
